D.C. Go-Go Music Foundation

The official music of Washington DC

Go-go was born in DC and never left. Chuck Brown shaped it in the early 1970s, and the pocket has been running in this city ever since.

The Fest Support the Foundation

Where it started

Go-go is the one major American genre that stayed in the city that made it. The history below is drawn from the Smithsonian and DC Preservation.

Early 1970s — Chuck Brown

Chuck Brown begins shaping go-go, building the continuous percussion groove that runs between songs without stopping.

The Maverick Room, 4th Street NE

The club where Brown first worked out the genre, identified by DC Preservation as go-go's origin site.

The circuit spreads

Clubs built for disco, funk and blues start booking the new bands. The Panorama Room in Anacostia, the Masonic Temple on U Street, the Coliseum in Northeast, RSVP and the Howard Theatre all become go-go rooms.

2020 — Official music of the District

Go-go is formally recognized as the official music of Washington DC.

The rooms

The stages that carried go-go, and the ones being rebuilt first for the 3D experience.

1215 U Street NW

Lincoln Theatre

Opened 1922, at the heart of a cultural renaissance that predated Harlem. A 38×40 foot proscenium stage and 1,225 seats.

620 T Street NW

Howard Theatre

Opened 1910. Chuck Brown performed here regularly; after his death thousands gathered out front.

4th Street NE

The Maverick Room

Where Chuck Brown first crafted go-go. The origin room.

Northwest

Metro Club — Deno's

Run by Daniel “Hollywood Breeze” Clayton. One of the longest-running go-go rooms in the city.

Anacostia

The Panorama Room

Southeast DC. Among the first clubs to take on the new sound.

U Street NW

The Masonic Temple

Part of the U Street corridor circuit as go-go spread across the city.
